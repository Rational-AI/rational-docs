# Conversations

**Conversations** is where staff review the conversations people have with your [touchpoints](./settings/touchpoints.mdx). Whenever someone talks to a touchpoint, the messages are captured here as a single conversation, with a title, topics and sentiment when those analyses are turned on. Use it to monitor activity, review what your assistants are saying, step into a live chat when a user needs a human, and audit individual messages down to the raw tool calls.

The page appears for anyone whose role has access to **Conversations**. A role that grants every conversation shows them all; otherwise you see only your own. Taking over a conversation, starring, generating a summary, refreshing labels and recording an oversight decision are for administrators. Chats with the agents of the [Agents](./Agents.mdx) section aren't listed here: they stay in **Agents** → **Chats**.

## Navigate your conversations

Open the **Conversations** section from the top navigation bar of the Control Room. The page presents a paginated table in which each row is one conversation. From here you can:

* **Search** across conversations using the search box in the page header.
* **Filter** the table from the **Filters** panel, and **sort** it by the **Last message** column.
* **Open a conversation** to read the full thread and take action.
* **Select rows** with their checkboxes to run bulk **Actions**. Hold **Shift** and select a second checkbox to select every row in between.

## The conversations table

Each row summarizes a single conversation across the following columns:

| Column                     | Description |
| :------------------------- | :---------- |
| **Conversation**           | The conversation title and a preview of the most recent message. Icons beside the title flag a conversation that **Needs review** or is **With an operator**. |
| **User and touchpoint**    | The end user who took part in the conversation, and the [touchpoint](./settings/touchpoints.mdx) it belongs to. |
| **Topics**                 | The topics detected for the conversation (for example, *Billing Request* or *Tech Support*). A conversation can carry several topics; the first two are shown as chips and the rest behind a **+N** chip. A conversation with no topics shows **None**. |
| **Sentiment**              | The estimated emotional tone of the conversation, shown as a colored dot with a label (for example, *Neutral*), or **Unavailable** when none was detected. |
| **Status**                 | The conversation's status, such as **Open** or **Closed**. |
| **Last message**           | The date and time of the most recent message. The table is sorted by this column, newest first, by default. |

Rows that need human oversight are tinted in the warning color, so they stand out without a column of their own.

The title, topics and sentiment shown here are produced by the automatic analyses you configure in [Conversations settings](./settings/conversations.mdx). Administrators can re-run them with **Refresh labels** after changing the topic list or models in settings.

## Filter the table

Select **Filters** next to the search box to open the filters panel beside the table. It offers:

* **User**, **Touchpoint** and **Topics**: select one or more values. The **User** list has its own search box, so you can find a person among everyone who has ever chatted.
* **Sentiment** and **Status**: pick one value.
* **Show only**: limit the table to **Favourites** or to conversations marked **Needs review**.

Choose **Apply** to filter the table, or **Clear** to empty the panel. The **Filters** button shows how many filters are active, and the applied filters are kept in the page address, so you can bookmark or share a filtered view.

## Open a conversation

Select any row (or choose **Open** from the row's **Actions** menu) to open the conversation. The table collapses into a narrow list beside the thread. Each assistant reply is labeled with who answered, and operator replies have their own style with a sent or failed state.

The conversation header gives you several tools:

* **Search** within the conversation.
* **Add to favourites** (the star icon) adds the conversation to your **Favourites**.
* **Details** opens the details drawer (see [The details drawer](#the-details-drawer)).
* **More conversation actions** holds **Advanced mode**, **Copy conversation ID** and **Delete conversation**. **Advanced mode** shows the technical detail behind each reply: tool calls and their responses, retrieval steps and any errors the assistant encountered. Use it to understand exactly what happened behind a reply.

### The details drawer

The **Details** drawer shows the facts about the conversation in one place: **User** (with a link to all of that user's conversations), **Touchpoint**, **Status**, **Sentiment**, **Started**, **Last message**, number of **Messages**, the conversation **ID**, its **Topics** (with a **Refresh labels** button) and, when summary generation is on, a **Summary**. Summaries aren't stored, so the drawer shows the ones generated during your session and offers **Generate summary** for a new one.

When a conversation is flagged for human oversight, the review panel sits at the end of this drawer.

### The take-over bar

While the assistant is in charge of a conversation, a bar replaces the message box at the bottom. It states who holds the conversation (for example, *"The AI handles this conversation"*, *"The AI is answering the user"* or *"Another operator is handling this conversation"*) and offers:

* **Take over:** hand the conversation from the assistant to you, so you can reply directly when a user needs personal attention. If someone got there first, you see *"Another operator has already taken over this conversation."*
* **Stop generating:** shown while the assistant is answering. It ends that answer without taking the conversation over.
* **Generate summary:** shown when summary generation is on. It opens an AI summary of the conversation for a quick recap of a long thread.

While you hold the conversation, **Hand back to the AI** returns it to the assistant.

For the full procedure, see [Take over an AI-managed conversation](/operational-guides/conversations#take-over-an-ai-managed-conversation).

## Conversations flagged for human oversight

When your oversight rules decide a conversation warrants human attention, it's marked **Needs review** in the table and a review panel appears at the end of the **Details** drawer. If the drawer is closed, a **Needs review** button in the conversation header opens it. The panel is where an administrator records what they concluded (**Leave as it is** or **I stepped in**) with an optional note.

Recording a decision resolves the flag, so the conversation leaves the review queue. The panel stays, collapsed to a single line naming the conclusion.

The rules that raise the flag are configured in [Conversations settings](./settings/conversations.mdx); the decision belongs on the conversation itself. For the full workflow, see [Record oversight decisions](/operational-guides/human-oversight).

## Best practices

* **Triage with filters:** combine the **Sentiment** and **Status** filters, or **Show only** conversations that need review, to surface conversations that may need attention.
* **Take over when it counts:** when an assistant is struggling or a user asks for a person, use **Take over** rather than letting the thread stall. If the assistant is only stuck on a runaway answer, **Stop generating** is enough.
* **Keep labels current:** after editing your topic list or switching models, select the affected conversations and **Refresh labels** so reporting stays accurate.
* **Debug with Advanced mode:** when a reply looks wrong, turn on **Advanced mode** to inspect the tool calls and errors behind it before changing a prompt or model.

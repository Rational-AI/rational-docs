# Sycophancy

**Sycophancy** is the tendency of a model to produce confidently agreeable answers, telling you what you seem to want to hear rather than what is most accurate. A sycophantic model may back down from a correct answer when challenged, validate a flawed assumption in your question, or praise a poor idea.

The cause lies in how models are aligned. During [reinforcement learning from human feedback](/concepts/RLHF), models are trained to favour responses that people rated highly, and people tend to rate agreeable, flattering answers more highly than blunt ones. The model learns that agreement is rewarded.

<Admonition type="note" icon="📝" title="Sycophancy vs hallucination">
A [hallucination](/concepts/hallucinations) is a confidently wrong statement of fact. Sycophancy is a confidently *agreeable* response shaped by what the user appears to want. The two often appear together: a leading question can nudge a model into agreeing with, and then fabricating support for, an incorrect premise.
</Admonition>

## How to guard against it

* **Ask neutrally:** avoid loading the question with the answer you expect. "Is this approach correct?" invites agreement; "What are the weaknesses of this approach?" invites scrutiny.
* **Ask for the case against:** explicitly request counterarguments or risks, not just confirmation.
* **Ground the answer:** anchoring responses in retrieved [Knowledge](/concepts/Knowledge) through [RAG](/concepts/RAG) gives the model verified material to lean on instead of your phrasing.
* **Keep a human in the loop:** for decisions that matter, a [human-in-the-loop](/concepts/Human-in-the-loop) review catches agreeable but wrong answers.

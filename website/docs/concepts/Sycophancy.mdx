# Sycophancy

**Sycophancy** is a model's tendency to agree with you or tell you what you want to hear, even when it's wrong. A sycophantic model backs down from a correct answer when you push back, accepts a false premise in your question, or praises a weak plan.

It comes from training. In [RLHF](/concepts/RLHF), people rate answers, and they tend to rate agreeable answers higher than blunt ones, so the model learns that agreeing pays. It often comes with [hallucinations](/concepts/hallucinations): a leading question can push a model to accept a wrong premise and then invent support for it.

## How to ask

- **Ask neutrally.** "Is this plan correct?" invites a yes. "What are the weak points of this plan?" invites a review.
- **Ask for the case against.** Request risks and counterarguments, not only confirmation.
- **Keep your hoped-for answer out of the question.** "Our supplier is reliable, right?" already contains the answer.

## In Rational AI

- An assistant that answers from your [Knowledge](/concepts/Knowledge) has sources to stand on, not only your wording. **Verify response** in Chat checks an answer against them. See [Check an answer against your knowledge](/operational-guides/chat-experience#check-an-answer-against-your-knowledge).
- For decisions that matter, have a person review the answer. See [Human-in-the-loop](/concepts/Human-in-the-loop).

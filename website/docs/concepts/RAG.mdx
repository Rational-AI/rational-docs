# Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)

**Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)** is how an assistant answers from your data. Before it writes an answer, it searches your [Knowledge](./Knowledge.mdx) for the passages most relevant to the question, and then writes the answer from them. The answer is grounded in your content, and the assistant can show which sources it used.

Without RAG, a model can only answer from what it learned in training, which knows nothing about your products, policies or records, and stops at its [knowledge cutoff](/reference/glossary#knowledge-cutoff).

## How it works

1. The question is turned into an [embedding](./Embeddings-chunks-and-annotations.mdx), a numerical representation of its meaning.
2. The Knowledge finds the passages whose meaning is closest to the question, even when they use different words.
3. The best passages that fit in the model's [context window](/reference/glossary#context-window) are handed to the model.
4. The model writes the answer from those passages.

## Two ways to search a Knowledge

An assistant can search a Knowledge in two ways. Both are [tools](/reference/glossary#tool) you turn on for a touchpoint.

| | Simple RAG | Graph RAG |
|---|---|---|
| What it finds | The passages closest in meaning to the question | Those passages, plus related ones connected to them in the Knowledge |
| Best for | Specific questions whose answer sits in one document | Broad questions where links between documents matter: citations, references, related products |
| Speed | Faster | Slower |
| Needs | Documents in the Knowledge | Relationships between the Knowledge's resources |

For example, asked *"What are the side effects of metformin?"*, Simple RAG returns the documents about metformin. Graph RAG also brings in what those documents are linked to, such as drug interactions or related studies.

Use Simple RAG by default. Turn on Graph RAG when your Knowledge has relationships and your users ask broad questions. See [Configure RAG](/operational-guides/configure-rag).

## When the assistant queries a database instead

A Knowledge can also hold structured data, such as tables of records. For questions about exact values, the assistant gets better answers by querying that data than by searching text:

- looking something up by its ID or exact name;
- counting, adding up or averaging ("how many orders in March?");
- filtering by numbers ("products under 50 euros").

An assistant can do both in one answer: search to find the right items, then query the data for the exact figures. Turn on only the tools that match what your Knowledge holds: database tools when it has structured data, search tools when it has documents.

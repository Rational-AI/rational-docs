# Human-in-the-loop

**Human-in-the-loop** is a way of working in which the agent proposes and a person approves before anything consequential happens. Instead of letting the model act on its own, a human stays in the decision path, reviewing, correcting or signing off on its output.

The reason is the nature of the model itself. Output is [non-deterministic](/concepts/Non-determinism), can be confidently wrong (a [hallucination](/concepts/hallucinations)) and can drift toward telling you what you want to hear ([sycophancy](/concepts/Sycophancy)). A human in the loop is the check that catches the cases automated grounding and review do not.

<Admonition type="note" icon="📝" title="A dial, not a switch">
Human-in-the-loop is not all-or-nothing. The right amount of oversight depends on the stakes: low-risk, easily reversible tasks may need little or none, while decisions that are costly, public or hard to undo warrant a human signing off every time.
</Admonition>

## When to keep a human in the loop

* **High stakes:** outputs that are costly, public-facing or hard to reverse.
* **Low confidence:** answers about recent or fast-changing facts, or topics near the model's [knowledge cutoff](/concepts/Knowledge-cutoff).
* **Sensitive judgement:** decisions that need accountability or domain expertise a model cannot supply.

## Human-in-the-loop in Rational AI

The platform is built so that AI work can be inspected rather than taken on trust. Grounding answers in your [Knowledge](/concepts/Knowledge) through [RAG](/concepts/RAG) narrows how far output can drift, and every conversation through a [touchpoint](/concepts/Touchpoint) can be reviewed, keeping a person in the loop wherever the stakes call for it.

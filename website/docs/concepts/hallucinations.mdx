# Hallucinations

A **hallucination** is an answer that sounds right but is false, or isn't supported by any source. The model isn't lying: it writes the most likely words for the question, and sometimes the most likely words are wrong.

## Why models hallucinate

- **They predict text, they don't check facts.** A model writes what usually follows, which reads well even when it's wrong.
- **Their knowledge is fixed.** A model knows only what was in its training data, up to its [knowledge cutoff](/reference/glossary#knowledge-cutoff), and nothing about your organization.
- **Vague questions leave gaps.** When a question is unclear, the model fills the gaps with plausible guesses.

## What they look like

| Type | Example |
| :--- | :--- |
| Wrong facts | A wrong date, name or figure: "The Eiffel Tower was built in 1867" (it was 1889). |
| Invented sources | A citation, a policy number or a link that doesn't exist. |
| Misread question | A question about Python the language answered about pythons the snakes. |
| Invented things | A product, a person or a rule that was never real. |

## How Rational AI reduces them

- **Answers from your data.** With [RAG](./RAG.mdx), an assistant searches your [Knowledge](./Knowledge.mdx) and answers from the passages it finds, and Chat shows the sources it used.
- **Checking an answer.** **Verify response** in Chat checks an answer against the retrieved knowledge and marks the sentences it can't support. See [Check an answer against your knowledge](/operational-guides/chat-experience#check-an-answer-against-your-knowledge).
- **Clear instructions.** A touchpoint's [system prompt](./System-prompt.mdx) can tell the assistant to say when it doesn't know.
- **A person in the loop.** For answers that matter, have someone review them before they're used. See [Human-in-the-loop](./Human-in-the-loop.mdx).

# Tokens

A **token** is the atomic unit of text a model reads and writes. Before a model can process your input, the text is broken into tokens; the model then generates its reply one token at a time. Tokens are the currency of everything a model does: limits, cost and speed are all measured in them.

A token is roughly word-sized, but not exactly. Common words are often a single token, while longer or rarer words are split into pieces. As a rough guide, one token is about four characters of English, so 1,000 tokens is roughly 750 words. Punctuation, spaces and line breaks count too.

<Admonition type="note" icon="📝" title="Tokens are not words">
Splitting text into tokens (called *tokenization*) is language- and model-specific. The same sentence can become a different number of tokens depending on the model, and non-English text often uses more tokens per word.
</Admonition>

## Why tokens matter

Almost every practical limit you meet comes back to tokens:

* **Context limits:** the [context window](/concepts/Context-window) is measured in tokens, and it is shared between the text you send in and the answer the model writes back.
* **Cost:** providers bill per token, and usually charge more for the tokens a model generates (output) than for the tokens you send (input).
* **Speed:** a model produces output token by token, so longer answers take longer to appear.

## Tokens in Rational AI

Wherever the platform talks about how much text a [model](/concepts/Model-architecture) can handle, or how much knowledge to retrieve for a request, it is ultimately budgeting tokens. Retrieving only the most relevant resources through [RAG](/concepts/RAG), rather than sending everything, keeps each request within the model's token budget and controls cost.

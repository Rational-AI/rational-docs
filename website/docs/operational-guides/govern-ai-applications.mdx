# Govern AI applications and provider costs

Once several teams start calling models through Rational AI, three questions get hard to answer: *which applications are actually using our AI*, *which of them should be allowed to*, and *what is each one really costing us*. The **Applications** tab answers the first, the governance settings you register enforce the second, and provider costs answer the third. This guide covers all three: taking inventory of the applications that reach your proxy, registering them with an owner, a status, and a risk tier that Rational AI enforces, requiring approval for models, and entering the pricing that turns raw token counts into costs.

:::note[What is a governed application?]
An **application** is whatever calls Rational AI through the proxy: an internal copilot, a batch job, a customer-facing assistant. Each call carries an **application key**, and Rational AI records it. A [touchpoint](/operational-guides/configure-touchpoints) is an application too: its conversations use the touchpoint's id as their key. Keys nobody has registered appear as **Unregistered**: they are counted and costed, but nobody has claimed them yet. **Registering** a key attaches the governance metadata (display name, owner, business unit, status, environment, risk tier, tags), and the status and risk tier decide whether and how the application is served.
:::

## Prerequisites

Before you start, make sure you have:

- **Administrative access:** the Governance area, the model approval policy, and the reporting currency are restricted to administrators.
- **Traffic through the proxy:** the inventory is built from observed requests, so a brand-new workspace lists nothing until something calls a model.
- **Provider pricing to hand** if you intend to complete the cost picture: your contract rates, subscription plans, or hosting costs.

## Take inventory of your applications

Open **Governance** and select the **Applications** tab. The page reads *Discovered from proxy traffic. Register them to assign ownership, risk, environment, and governance tags*, a reminder that nothing here was declared up front: Rational AI found it.

1. If any key has not been registered, a callout at the top says how many applications use models through the proxy without being governed. Select **Show unregistered** to list only those.
2. Narrow the list with the status switch: **All**, **Approved**, **Pending review**, **Blocked**, or **Unregistered**. Each option shows its count.
3. To find a specific application, type in the search box. It matches the name, key, owner, or a tag.

Each row tells you one application's story:

- **Application**: the display name, the raw key beneath it, and a **Touchpoint** badge when the key belongs to a touchpoint.
- **Status**: **Pending review**, **Approved**, **Blocked**, or **Unregistered** for a key that was never claimed.
- **Risk**, **Owner**, **Environment**, and **Tags**: the governance metadata you registered.
- **Requests**, **Tokens**, and **Cost**: what it consumed in the selected period, with the cost in the reporting currency.

The actions menu on each row offers **Edit** (or **Register** for an unregistered key), **See its requests**, which opens the **Audit log** filtered to that application, **Copy key**, and **Delete** for a registration.

:::note[The inventory follows the period]
The **Requests**, **Tokens**, and **Cost** columns cover the **Period** set in the Governance filters. Widen the period and the same application shows larger numbers; it is the same application either way.
:::

## Register an application

Register a key to turn an anonymous string in the inventory into something with an owner you can ask about it.

1. On the **Applications** tab, select **Register application**, or select **Register** in the actions menu of an unregistered row, which fills in its key. The form opens in a panel docked beside the list.
2. Fill in the **Application key**. It *must match the application tag sent to the Rational AI proxy*, character for character: this is the join between the registration and the traffic. For a touchpoint, the key is the touchpoint's id.
3. Add a **Display name**, and set the **Owner**: a team or a person.
4. Optionally add a **Business unit**.
5. Choose the **Status**: **Pending review** (the default), **Approved**, or **Blocked**.
6. Choose the **Environment**: **Development**, **Test**, **Staging**, or **Production** (the default).
7. Choose the **Risk tier**: **Low**, **Limited** (the default), **High**, or **Unacceptable**. The panel explains what the chosen tier does for this application.
8. Add **Tags**, pressing Enter after each one, for example `customer-facing`, `pii`, `critical`.
9. Optionally add a **Description**.
10. Select **Save application**.

To change any of this later, select **Edit** in the row's actions menu. The same panel opens with the application's usage in the period and a link to its requests.

## Decide what each application may do

The status and the risk tier are not labels: Rational AI checks them every time a model request is admitted, whether it comes from a chat, a touchpoint's API, or a direct call through the proxy.

| Setting | What happens to new requests |
|---|---|
| Status **Pending review** or **Approved** | Served normally. The status records your review. |
| Status **Blocked** | Refused. Every new model request sent with the application's key gets an error. For a touchpoint, every new conversation turn gets an error. |
| Risk tier **Low** or **Limited** | Served normally. The tier is recorded. |
| Risk tier **High** | On a touchpoint, every tool call waits for a person's approval, whatever the touchpoint's own approval setting, and callers of its API are offered no tools. On any other application, the tier is recorded only. |
| Risk tier **Unacceptable** | Refused, like a blocked application. |

:::warning[Blocking an application stops it at once]
A block applies to the next request. Requests and conversation turns already running finish, but nothing new is served until you change the status back. Before you block a touchpoint, make sure nobody depends on it right now.
:::

:::note[High risk is enforced on touchpoints only]
Rational AI can put a person in front of a tool call only where it runs the tools itself, which is in a touchpoint's conversations. An application that calls models directly runs its own tools, so for it a **High** tier is a record of your assessment, not a control. See [Approve tool calls before they run](/operational-guides/approve-tool-calls) for what the approval looks like.
:::

A refused request gets an HTTP 403 error whose body carries a `code`, so a caller can tell a governance decision from a provider failure. In the chat, the turn says why it was refused instead of showing a generic error.

| Code | Reason |
|---|---|
| `application-blocked` | The application is registered as **Blocked**. |
| `application-unacceptable-risk` | The application's risk tier is **Unacceptable**. |
| `model-withdrawn` | The model's approval was withdrawn. |
| `model-not-approved` | The model has not been approved and the workspace requires approval. |
| `model-disabled` | An administrator disabled the model. |

Every registration, change, and deletion of an application is written to the [audit trail](/operational-guides/audit-trail), and a change of status or risk tier is named in the entry.

## Require approval for models

Applications are one side of the decision; the models they use are the other. Each model has a governance record where an administrator documents it and approves it (see [Document and approve a model](/operational-guides/ai-model-registry-features#document-and-approve-a-model)). The model approval policy decides whether that approval is required.

1. Go to **Settings** → **AI Models**.
2. In the **Model approval** card, read the current state. It names the models that have not been reviewed, each a link to its details.
3. Switch on **Require approval**.
4. Read the confirmation. It lists the models that have not been approved and will refuse every request (chats, API calls, and knowledge processing that use them) until someone approves them.
5. Select **Require approval** to confirm.

| Model approval state | Approval not required (default) | Approval required |
|---|---|---|
| **Approved** | Answers | Answers |
| **Not reviewed** | Answers | Refused |
| **Withdrawn** | Refused | Refused |

While approval is required, the model list shows an **Approval** column that turns red for a model that refuses requests, and model pickers offer only models that can answer, so nobody configures a model that would then be refused.

:::warning[Editing an approved model can stop it]
Editing a substantive field of an approved model's governance record revokes its approval. With **Require approval** on, that model then refuses every request until it is approved again. The model's details warn you before such a save.
:::

Approving a model, an approval revoked by an edit, and every change to the policy are written to the [audit trail](/operational-guides/audit-trail).

## Tag a request with its application

Rational AI attributes a request to an application in one of two ways, in this order:

1. **The `X-RationalAI-Application` header** sent by the caller. The header always wins.
2. **The proxy's default application key**, used when the caller sends no header. You set it on the third-party model in the [AI Model Registry](/operational-guides/ai-model-registry-features): the **Default application key** field (for example `claims-assistant`), described there as *used to attribute every request through this proxy when the caller does not send `X-RationalAI-Application`*.

Set the default key on any proxy whose callers you don't control, and the traffic still lands under a name instead of under nothing. A request with neither shows as **Direct** in the **Audit log**.

## Export the inventory

When someone outside Rational AI needs the list (a review meeting, a compliance pack, a spreadsheet), export it.

1. On the **Applications** tab, select **Export**.
2. A CSV is downloaded, named `governed-ai-applications-` plus today's date.

The **Audit log** tab has its own **Export** for the request-by-request record; it is disabled when the current filters return no rows.

## Price what you are using

Token counts only become costs once Rational AI knows what your providers charge. When requests have no price, the **Needs attention** panel on the **Overview** says so, and **Set prices** opens the **Costs** tab. Its **Rate history** section lists each model with the rate in force and its coverage, flagging gaps as unpriced requests; open a model there to see, correct, or backdate the prices it was charged at.

### Model prices

A model's price is not always "so much per million tokens", so the price editor covers the shapes providers actually bill in:

- The **currency** the provider charges in. Costs are converted into your reporting currency for display, and the original amount is kept.
- **Token dimensions**: input, output, and cached input priced separately.
- **Per-request**, **per-image**, and **web-search** components.
- **Time-based billing** for self-hosted models and reserved capacity, charged by the hour, minute, or second and derived from how long the deployment was actually up.

For providers that publish a catalog, prices can be imported instead of typed: an **OpenRouter** connector can synchronize model pricing on demand, and manual and contract overrides you entered yourself are preserved rather than overwritten.

### Provider subscriptions

Some spend does not vary per call at all: a monthly API plan, a yearly enterprise token pool. Model it as a subscription on the provider's connector. **Provider subscriptions** on the **Overview** opens the subscriptions of your busiest connector in **Settings** → **Connectors**.

1. Open the provider's connector in **Settings** → **Connectors** and add a **Provider subscription**.
2. Enter the recurring **amount** and its period (**Monthly** or **Yearly**), and the dates it **starts** and (optionally) **ends**.
3. Choose the **Cost allocation**, for example **Allocate by application usage**, which spreads the fixed fee across applications in proportion to what they used.
4. Under **Included usage and overage**, add an allowance per usage type: **All tokens** or **Requests**, how much is **Included**, the **Scale** (units, million, billion), and the **Overage** rate charged beyond it, per million tokens or per unit for requests.

Each allowance resets at the start of the billing period. If the provider does not publish an overage rate, leave the field empty rather than guessing: an invented rate quietly turns into an invented cost on the Overview.

## Choose the reporting currency

Providers bill in different currencies, and adding dollars to euros gives a number nobody can use. Governance therefore reports every cost in a single currency, euros unless you choose another.

1. Open **Governance** and select the **Costs** tab.
2. Select **Reported in** at the top right. The list shows the currencies there are exchange rates for, and the date of the latest rates.
3. Choose a currency.

Every governance figure is shown in that currency: the totals, cost composition, the applications' **Cost** column, the audit log, and the cost charts. Rates and invoices you entered keep their original currency.

:::note[Costs are converted at the rate of the day they were incurred]
Each cost is converted at the European Central Bank euro reference rate of the day it was incurred, or the latest rate published before that day, which covers weekends and holidays. Hover over a total to see the original amounts and how they were converted; on a single request, the tooltip names the rate and its date.

A cost is never converted at a guessed rate. If there is no rate for its day, for example because the installation cannot reach the internet to download the rates, the amount stays in its own currency and is shown next to the converted total.
:::

## Check how complete your costs are

Pricing is never finished all at once, so Governance tells you how much of it is done. The **Governance health** card on the **Overview** reports **Cost coverage** as a percentage and **Unpriced requests** as a count: requests that ran, that you paid for in the real world, and that Rational AI cannot cost because the model has no price. To see them, open the **Audit log** and select **Unpriced**. Fill in the prices and the coverage figure moves toward 100%, at which point the **Total costs** headline is worth quoting to a finance team.

The same card also shows the **Error rate** and the **Cache hit rate** for the period, so a spike in spend can be read next to a spike in retries.

## Result

You have an inventory of every application calling your AI, touchpoints included, each registered with an owner, an environment, a risk tier, and tags. **Blocked** and **Unacceptable** applications are refused, **High** risk touchpoints wait for a person on every tool call, and with **Require approval** on, only approved models answer. Requests are attributed by the `X-RationalAI-Application` header or the proxy's default key, and provider pricing (per-token, per-request, time-based, subscriptions, or synchronized from a catalog) turns the token counts into costs in one reporting currency, with **Cost coverage** telling you how much of the picture is still missing.

# Monitor usage, cost, and audit logs

Once your AI is in use, you'll want to know how much it's being used, what it's costing you, how fast it responds, and exactly what happened on any given request. The **Governance** area gives you all of that in one place, and it tells you first what needs your attention.

:::note[What is governance?]
Governance is a top-navigation area (alongside Chat, Knowledge, Conversations, and [BI](/operational-guides/rational-bi)) that reports on your AI's usage and holds the decisions about who may use it. It has five tabs: **Overview** for what needs attention and the headline figures, **Usage** for the charts over time, **Applications** for the inventory of what is calling your AI, **Audit log** for the request-by-request record, and **Costs** for the cost ledger.
:::

Before going further, two terms show up everywhere in this area:

- An **inference** is a single call to a model: one question in, one answer out.
- A **token** is the small chunk of text that models read and write, and the unit they're billed by. A short sentence is a handful of tokens; a long document is thousands. (For more on the models behind these calls, see [LLMs](/concepts/LLMs).)

## Prerequisites

- **Administrative access:** the Governance area is restricted to administrators.
- **Traffic through the proxy:** the figures are built from the model requests that Rational AI captures, so a new workspace shows little until something calls a model.

## Start from the Overview

Open **Governance**. You land on the **Overview** tab, which reads top to bottom: what needs action, then the headline figures, then the context behind them.

1. If anything is wrong, a **Needs attention** panel sits at the top, collapsed to one line that counts the problems, for example *1 unregistered application · 412 unpriced requests*. Select **Show** to list them. Each problem has a button that takes you straight to the fix:

   | Problem | Button | Where it takes you |
   |---|---|---|
   | Applications seen in proxy traffic that nobody has registered | **Register apps** | **Applications**, narrowed to the unregistered ones |
   | Requests whose model has no price | **Set prices** | **Costs** |
   | A high error rate | **See requests** | **Audit log**, narrowed to the failed requests |
   | Workflow runs that failed or timed out | **See the runs** | **Audit log** → **Workflow runs** |

2. Read the totals for the period: **Total costs** leads, followed by **Total inferences**, **Total input tokens**, and **Total output tokens**.
3. Beside the featured chart, check **Cost composition**, the costs recorded for the period by source with their total, and **Governance health**: **Cost coverage** (how much of your usage Rational AI can price), **Unpriced requests**, **Error rate**, and **Cache hit rate**.
4. At the foot, **Top applications** lists the busiest applications by requests, with their status, environment, tokens, and cost. **View all** opens the **Applications** tab.

:::note[Needs attention only appears when something is wrong]
When every application is registered, every request is priced, and nothing is failing, the panel is not shown at all. An Overview that opens on the totals is a healthy one.
:::

## Follow trends on the Usage tab

The **Usage** tab holds the charts. Choose the traffic type with the switch at the top:

- **APIs** shows model traffic through the proxy, grouped into **Volume** (tokens and inferences), **Speed** (average tokens per second and average time to first token), and **Unit economics** (costs, model cost per inference, and cost per model).
- **Conversational** shows the chats on your [touchpoints](/operational-guides/configure-touchpoints), grouped into **Engagement** (active users, active conversations, and user messages) and **Quality** (topics and sentiment).

Hover over a chart to read the value for a single day or time bucket.

## Filter what you're looking at

Filters live in a panel on the right, so they stay out of the way until you need them.

1. Select **Filters** in the toolbar. The button shows how many filters are active.
2. Set the filters you need. The panel lists only the filters that change the open tab:
   - **Touchpoints**, **Models**, and **Applications**: up to four of each. They apply to **Overview**, **Usage**, and the request list in **Audit log**. **Applications** is not available for conversational usage.
   - **Period**: *Last 24 hours*, *Last 7 days*, *Last 30 days* (the default), *Last 3 months*, *Last 6 months*, *Last year*, or *All*. It applies to every tab except **Costs**.
3. Select **Apply**. Nothing reloads until you do, so you can change several filters and pay for a single refresh.

To start over, select **Clear**. It resets the filters of the open tab and leaves the others for the tabs that use them.

These filters are how you turn a broad overview into a real answer. Want to know which touchpoint is driving spend? Set **Period** to the last 30 days and filter one touchpoint at a time while you watch the **Costs** chart on **Usage**. Suspect one model is slow? Filter to it and check **Average time to first token**.

## Inspect individual requests in the audit log

When you need to look past the averages at a single request, open the **Audit log** tab. It lists every model request made through the Rational AI proxy, newest first, within the current filters.

:::warning[Audit log is not the audit trail]
Two similarly-named surfaces answer different questions:

- **Audit log** (this tab): one row per **model request**, with the model, tokens, cost, and speed. It's about *the AI doing work*.
- **[Audit trail](/operational-guides/audit-trail)** (**Settings** → **Audit trail**): one row per **access or change to a record**, such as who opened a conversation or who changed a dashboard. It's about *people reaching data*.

If someone asks you to evidence who looked at a record, this tab is not the answer. The audit trail is.
:::

1. Choose which requests to list with the switch at the top: **All**, **Errors** (requests that did not end with a success status, as the **Error rate** counts them), or **Unpriced** (requests without a complete cost, as **Unpriced requests** counts them).
2. Read the columns:
   - **Timestamp**: when the request was made.
   - **Model**: which model handled it.
   - **Application**: the [governed application](/operational-guides/govern-ai-applications) the request was attributed to, or **Direct** when it carried no application.
   - **Tokens**: the count in, then out (input → output).
   - **Costs**: what the request cost, in the reporting currency. Hover over it to see the original amount and the exchange rate used.
   - **TTFT**: *time to first token*, how long until the first piece of the answer came back. This is what a user experiences as the wait before a response starts.
   - **E2E latency**: *end-to-end latency*, the total time from request to the full response.
   - **Speed**: generation speed in tokens per second.
   - **Finish**: why the response ended: **Completed**, **Truncated**, **Tool calls**, or **Filtered**.
3. Select a column header to sort. Sorting by **Costs** surfaces your most expensive requests; sorting by **TTFT** or **E2E latency** brings the slowest ones to the top.
4. Select a row to open the request in a panel docked beside the list. The panel groups what was captured:
   - **Request**: the time, the status, and the operation (for example **Chat completion** or **Embedding**).
   - **Source**: the model, application, touchpoint, user, and session.
   - **Usage and cost**: tokens in and out, and the cost. A request with no price reads **Not priced**, with a shortcut to set the model's rate.
   - **Performance**: TTFT, E2E latency, and speed.
   - **Request body** and **Response body**: the payloads, when the model's tracing recorded them. Otherwise the panel says the payload was not recorded and links to the model's tracing settings.

:::note[Payloads are recorded only when tracing says so]
A model records request and response payloads only when its tracing settings say so, and by default it never does. How long recorded payloads live is governed by [your retention policy](/operational-guides/data-retention).

The values of credential headers are redacted before a request is written. The header *name* is kept, so you can still see that a request carried an `Authorization` header, but the token itself never reaches the database.
:::

:::note[TTFT vs E2E latency]
These two measure different kinds of "slow." A high **TTFT** means the model took a while to *start* answering, and the user stares at a blank screen. A high **E2E latency** with a low TTFT usually means a long answer that streamed steadily. When a touchpoint feels sluggish, check TTFT first.
:::

The list keeps itself current: new requests appear as they are captured, without reloading the page. To take the record elsewhere, select **Export** to download every row that matches your current filters as a CSV. The button is disabled when the filters return nothing to export.

### Review workflow runs

Switch the **Audit log** from **Requests** to **Workflow runs** to see each run of your [workflows](/operational-guides/create-workflow) in the period, with **Started**, **Status**, **Workflow**, **Duration**, and **Failure**. Select a run to open its details in the docked panel, including the current step and the error code of a failed run. When your plan meters workflow runtime, the top of this view shows the minutes used this month.

## Review costs in the cost ledger

The **Costs** tab, titled **Cost ledger**, holds every rate, invoice, and asset that feeds the cost figures. It shows where your costs come from (**Infrastructure runtime**, **Facility and operations**, **Amortised assets**, and **Provider subscriptions**), and has a section for each kind of entry: **Rate history**, **Booked costs**, **Amortised assets**, and **Node pools**.

All governance costs are shown in a single reporting currency, which you choose with **Reported in** at the top right of this tab. See [Govern AI applications](/operational-guides/govern-ai-applications#choose-the-reporting-currency) for how costs are converted.

:::note[Old dashboard and audit log links still work]
Governance used to open on a **Dashboard** tab next to an **Audit Log** tab. Bookmarks and shared links to those tabs still work: the Dashboard opens **Overview**, and the Audit Log opens the request list in **Audit log**.
:::

## Result

You start from the **Overview**, where **Needs attention** points you to anything that needs fixing and the totals summarize the period. The **Usage** tab shows the trends, the filter panel scopes every tab to the touchpoints, models, applications, and period you care about, and the **Audit log** shows each request in a docked panel, with its cost, speed, and, when tracing allows, its payloads. To turn the application names in these tables into an owned, priced inventory, continue with [Govern AI applications](/operational-guides/govern-ai-applications).

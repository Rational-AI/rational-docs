# Review flagged conversations and record the decision

Oversight rules flag conversations that a person should look at. Flagging is only half of it: what an auditor asks isn't *was this raised for review?* but **did somebody look, what did they conclude, and did they step in?** This guide covers recording that decision on the conversation, and why the record — not the flag — is the thing that survives.

<Admonition type="info" icon="📖" title="THE FLAG ASKS THE QUESTION, THE DECISION ANSWERS IT">
A conversation carries a flag when your oversight rules decide it warrants human attention. The flag is a queue: it says *someone should look at this*.

The **decision** is what a reviewer records once they have. It names the conclusion, who reached it, when, and optionally why. "The system flags cases for a human" is a claim; the decision is what makes it demonstrable.
</Admonition>

## Prerequisites

Before you start, make sure you have:

- **Administrative access:** recording an oversight decision is admin-gated.
- **Oversight rules already configured.** The rules that raise the flag live in **Settings → Conversations**; without them nothing is ever flagged, and this page has nothing to act on.

## Where the decision goes

Open a flagged conversation. A bar sits at the **top of the conversation itself** — on the case, where the reviewer already is, rather than in a separate queue screen they'd have to go back to.

That split is intentional: the **rules** are configuration and live in **Settings → Conversations**; the **decision** belongs on the thing being decided.

## Record a decision

Read the conversation, then choose one of two outcomes and optionally add a note:

| Outcome | What it means |
|---------|---------------|
| **Leave as it is** | You reviewed it and concluded no action was needed. |
| **I stepped in** | You reviewed it and intervened. |

Both are decisions a reviewer took. Leaving a conversation to stand is a conclusion, not a non-event — which is why neither option reads as dismissing a notification.

The note is optional but is the only place the *reasoning* is captured. If the conclusion would puzzle someone reading it in six months, write the note.

## Once decided, the record stays visible

Recording a decision **resolves the flag** — the conversation leaves the queue of things waiting for a human, which is what you want, since it has now had one.

The bar doesn't disappear, though. It collapses to a single line: what was concluded, when, and the note.

<Admonition type="note" icon="📝" title="THAT ONE LINE IS THE WHOLE POINT">
Resolving the flag used to be all that happened, and it destroyed the information: the conversation simply dropped out of the queue and nothing anywhere said a review had occurred.

Now the decision is what remains after the flag clears. Lowering the flag no longer loses the fact that somebody looked.
</Admonition>

## You cannot un-review a conversation

There's no option that returns a reviewed conversation to *awaiting review*. Pending is the **absence** of a review, not a conclusion someone can reach.

Accepting it as a decision would hand a reviewer a way to un-review a case while leaving a timestamp behind saying they had looked — which is worse than never having recorded anything.

## Result

You can close the loop on a flagged conversation by recording what you concluded, right on the case. You know the decision resolves the flag while leaving a durable line saying who decided what and when, that the note is the only place your reasoning is captured, and that a review can't be undone — because the absence of a review is not something anyone can decide.
